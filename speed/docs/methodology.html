Vehicle Speed & 3D Localization

Methodology and mathematical formulation for monocular vehicle speed estimation from fixed cameras at arbitrary viewing angles

Two calibration modes — oblique (depth-assisted) and overhead (closed-form). Validated on four scenes. All empirical figures measured, not estimated.

Part I — Methodology

1. The original approach and why it failed

  • METERS_PER_PIXEL = 0.05 collapsed all of projective geometry into one constant. Valid only when the ground is fronto-parallel at a fixed distance — a nadir drone shot at known altitude.
  • It fails on oblique views because metres-per-pixel varies across the frame: a car near the horizon covers several times more ground per pixel than one in the foreground.
  • Speed came from a 3-frame pixel difference, which differentiates tracker jitter straight into the output.
  • Over-speed readings were silently zeroed (if speed > MAX: speed = 0), making a tracker glitch indistinguishable from a parked car.
  • The constant was also wrong even for nadir: calibration measured 0.034 m/px against the hand-tuned 0.05 — a ~47% overestimate.

2. Core decision: geometry, not per-frame depth

  • Speed is a derivative. Per-frame depth networks flicker, and differencing amplifies that noise. Depth-based speed methods land near 3.6 km/h RMSE; geometric calibration reaches ~1.1 km/h.
  • So: recover scene geometry once, then use exact projective math every frame. Vehicles sit on a road; the road is a plane. Given intrinsics and pose relative to that plane, a pixel back-projects to an exact 3D point.
  • Consequence: no neural depth in the runtime loop, no flicker, and residual error is a systematic bias that can be calibrated out rather than random noise that cannot.
  • Distance from camera needs no depth model at all — it falls out of the ray/plane intersection as ‖P‖.

3. Camera model and the plane

  • OpenCV convention: X right, Y down, Z forward, camera at origin.
  • Ground plane is {P : n·P = −h} — n the unit up-vector in camera coordinates, h the camera height above it.
  • Intersection is valid only when n·d < 0. Rays at or above the horizon never meet the plane and are rejected rather than clamped.
  • World frame: origin at camera nadir, +Z up, so ground points land at z=0 and the camera sits at (0,0,h).

4. Calibration — fixed / oblique camera

  • Focal length ← Depth Pro's FOV head. This is the only reason the large model is used. Not its metric depth.
  • An attempt to replace it failed. solve_focal() searched for the focal that makes the road planar. It is structurally broken: as f→∞ every point collapses onto a line, which is trivially planar, so the residual falls monotonically and the search runs away. On a clip of known focal 1589 px it returned the 5760 px ceiling and a 7–8° tilt against a 23.75° vanishing-point truth. Retained in code, labelled unreliable, rather than deleted — the negative result is worth recording.
  • Plane orientation ← RANSAC on the depth point cloud. Only the unit normal is kept; a normalized vector is invariant to a wrong global scale, which matters because the depth model's absolute scale is out of distribution on aerial or unusual views.
  • Road mask is anchored to vehicles — a dilated band around detected boxes, minus the boxes. Fitting to "where cars actually are" keeps the plane off rooftops and canopy.
  • Plane fit uses the 3×3 covariance, not np.linalg.svd(P). numpy's SVD defaults to full_matrices=True, which for an (N,3) cloud allocates an (N,N) factor — 14 GB at N=42k, 320 GB at N=200k. This, not the depth model, was what crashed the machine twice.
  • Two frames suffice. The camera is static, so focal and tilt are identical every frame, and one frame's road mask already yields ~10⁵ points.

5. Calibration — drone / nadir camera

  • The normal is known exactly: n = [0,0,−1]. Nothing to estimate.
  • No depth model is loaded at all — and running one would be worse than useless. Plane tilt is recovered from the depth gradient, which is ~zero looking straight down, so a fit has no signal and converts the model's own error into tilt that is not there. Amplified by f/(W/2) ≈ 1.67×, so a 3% depth error becomes ~3° of false tilt.
  • Depth is genuinely constant. Verified on real footage: 65.20 m at frame centre, 65.20 m at the corners; only slant range varies (to 70.94 m).
  • Focal is degenerate with altitude — only the ratio h/f is observable, so ground positions and speeds are exact for any chosen f.
  • This is why a single metres-per-pixel constant is correct overhead — and only overhead. The original approach worked there for a real reason.

6. Scale — the physical anchor

  • Absolute scale never comes from a network. It comes from car width against 1.83 m, a physical constant.
  • Plus a body-height term. The box's lateral extremities sit on the car body ~0.75 m up, not on the road. Omitting that term cost a 16% scale error; restoring it moved measured lane width from 3.18 m to 3.59 m against a 3.7 m reference.
  • 25th percentile, not median. Length contamination can only inflate the measurement, so the samples have a hard floor at the truth with a tail above it — the median sits inside the contamination.
  • The perpendicularity gate alone cannot fix it: since L ≫ W, even 85° off admits 4.5(0.087) + 1.83(0.996) = 2.21 m, still 21% over.
  • But the raw minimum is far worse — jitter corrupts the bottom tail. Scored against lane markings: min +140%, p5 +12%, p25 +2%, median −6%.

7. Runtime measurement chain

  • Ray through the box centre, intersected at z = 0.75 m — not the box bottom. In an oblique view the box bottom is the tyre contact line, but in a nadir view the box is the roof outline and its "bottom" is meaningless. Centroid-height intersection is approximately correct in both regimes with no branch.
  • Velocity from a windowed least-squares fit over 0.6 s — not frame differencing. Handles irregular dt, gives heading free, transparent to debug.
  • Timestamps from a real clock or media position, never a frame counter — the live path drops frames to stay current.

8. Uncertainty

  • Precision falls off linearly with distance, not quadratically. The naive derivation gives d²/(h·f) per pixel of wobble — true for a fixed pixel jitter. But detector jitter scales with box size, and a car's box shrinks as 1/d, cancelling one power of d.
  • Measured on 114 tracked vehicles spanning 20–160 m, scatter scales as d^1.00. The quadratic form over-predicted noise by 5–20× and withheld most of a motorway — 35% of readings reported versus 92% once corrected.
  • No 1/√n averaging term. Detection bias is correlated across neighbouring frames, so extra samples inside one short window buy no precision. Assuming independence underestimated real noise threefold.
  • A reading must clear 2σ to be claimed. 3σ was measurably too strict.
Three deliberately distinct output states. N km/h — measured and resolvable · 0.0 km/h (stopped) — genuinely stationary, a valid reading · UNRELIABLE — no claim made. Collapsing the third into the second was the original bug: a parked car and a tracker glitch must stay tellable apart.

9. Detection

  • The camera mode selects the detector, not just the geometry. A COCO model barely recognises a car from directly overhead — measured on the drone clip, yolo11s found 0 vehicles/frame at conf 0.25 and 1–2 at conf 0.10, while visDrone found 23–28.
  • yolo11s over yolo11m for oblique — benchmarked on real footage it is 2× faster (34 vs 16 fps) and finds more vehicles (15 vs 10). The larger model is more conservative on distant, low-contrast frames. Not a tradeoff.
  • Class filtering matches on name, not index, so it survives a model swap.

10. Ego-motion

  • Drift correction for hovering drones: ORB + RANSAC homography back to the calibration reference, computed on background only (vehicle boxes masked out) so moving traffic cannot drag the estimate.
  • Gated in both directions: skipped below ~1 px (a static camera, where fitting only injects match noise) and rejected above ~50 px (a bad match, not drift).
  • Why it matters: 2 px of hover drift at 24 fps manufactures a phantom speed for every parked car.

11. Input handling — the layer that actually broke

Every failure in this project came from here, not from the geometry. All were silent: the pipeline ran, produced confident, plausible-looking numbers, and nothing announced that an input assumption had been violated.

  • YouTube's best format is 360p. It only considers progressive muxed streams, which YouTube caps at 640×360. A 720p video silently arrived downscaled, placing the horizon above the frame and reporting motorway traffic at 12–16 km/h. Fixed by requesting video-only (bv*).
  • Live vs recorded need opposite treatment. Live: keep the newest frame, drop stale ones, stamp with the wall clock. Recorded: keep every frame with backpressure, stamp by media position, pace to real time. A finite video decodes ~42× faster than real time.
  • Replay needs monotonic stamps and history clearing. Without both, tracks straddling the loop boundary get a near-zero dt and an absurd speed — observed at 97 km/h for a car doing about 46.
  • Calibration clips are keyed per source. They were a single shared filename with "reuse if it exists" logic, so only the first source ever recorded one and every later video was calibrated from the first one's footage — three unrelated clips came out with byte-identical calibrations and depth maps.

12. Resource discipline

  • One model on the GPU at a time. The detector is explicitly freed before the depth model loads. That overlap is what exhausts a 24 GB unified-memory machine.
  • MPS watermarks set before torch initialises (LOW=0.5, HIGH=0.6 — and both must be set, since the low default of 1.4 must not exceed the high) so torch raises a catchable OOM instead of taking the OS down.
  • fp16 on MPS is not a compromise: against a CPU fp32 reference the focal matched to 0.06% with identical depth percentiles, at 5× the speed and half the memory.
  • Depth Pro's input is fixed at 1536×1536 (patch_size 384 × ratios [0.25,0.5,1]) — not reducible, so the cost is ~7 GB regardless of source resolution.

13. Serving

  • stdlib http.server + MJPEG multipart — one endpoint needs no framework.
  • Deliberately HTTP/1.0: an MJPEG body is endless with no Content-Length; under HTTP/1.1 a client expects a length or chunked encoding, gets neither, and treats the response as malformed.
  • The stream element self-heals. An MJPEG <img> never reconnects on its own, so it attaches only while visible and re-arms on error. Reconnect URLs carry a cache-buster, which means routes must match on path, not the whole request string.
  • Reader threads join before releasing the capture. Calling VideoCapture.release() while another thread is inside read() segfaults the interpreter with no traceback.

14. Validation method

  • Never validate against intuition; validate against a measurable reference. An early assumption that one clip was a "60–110 km/h highway" was wrong and sent the investigation hunting a non-existent bug for a long time. It was an urban arterial.
  • References used: the lane-marking vanishing point (tilt agreed to 0.6°), lane width against 3.7 m / 3.65 m standards (+2%), the visible horizon row versus the calibrated horizon_row (206 vs ~199), and parked cars that must read ~0.
  • Cross-validation across independent paths: two cameras, two calibration methods (depth-based oblique, model-free nadir), converging on the same physical answer.
  • The end-to-end check reports NOT EXERCISED when a clip contains no stationary vehicle, rather than passing vacuously.
  • Ground truth is taken from each track's own trajectory, never from the speed the pipeline reported — otherwise the check merely confirms its own answer.

15. Known limits

  • The focal head is a single point of failure with no cross-check. Every silent failure was diagnosed by hand-comparing horizon_row to the visible horizon. Automating that comparison is the highest-value remaining addition.
  • Single flat plane — hills, banked curves and multi-level junctions all violate it.
  • No lens distortion model — wide-angle and security cameras carry systematic error toward the frame edges.
  • The camera must not move; any pan/tilt/zoom silently invalidates calibration and nothing detects it.
  • Detection is the real floor — night footage found almost nothing regardless of how good the geometry was.
  • Evidence base is four scenes, all fixed cameras (three oblique, one nadir). That is not a broad sample.
  • At 110–160 m the noise model sits at 0.66× — slightly optimistic at extreme range.

17. Shared core — both modes

Back-projection ray (unnormalised, Z-component 1):

d(u,v) = [ (u−cx)/f ,  (v−cy)/f ,  1 ]ᵀ

Ground plane, and the plane at height z above it:

n · P = −h                 n · P = −(h − z)

Ray–plane intersection. Substituting P = t·d:

n·(t d) = −(h−z)     ⟹     t = −(h − z) / (n · d)

P = t · d                                    [camera frame]

Validity. The ray meets the plane in front of the camera only when

n · d < 0

Horizon row. Setting n·d = 0 at u = cx:

v_horizon = cy − f · n_z / n_y

World transform. Build an orthonormal basis from n:

ŵ = normalise( ẑ − (ẑ·n) n ),   ẑ = [0,0,1]ᵀ     (fallback ŷ if degenerate)
r̂ = ŵ × n

X = [ P·r̂ ,  P·ŵ ,  P·n + h ]ᵀ

Ground points land at x_z = 0; the camera sits at (0,0,h). Slant distance, reported as "distance from camera", is D = ‖P‖.

18. Mode A — fixed / oblique camera

Here both n and f are estimated.

18.1 Intrinsics

f = (W_img / 2) / tan(FOV_h / 2),    cx = W_img/2,   cy = H_img/2

18.2 Ground-plane normal

With D(u,v) the metric depth, back-project road-masked pixels into a cloud:

𝒫 = { D(u,v) · d(u,v)  :  (u,v) ∈ M_road }

M_road = dilate(⋃ boxes, 41) \ (⋃ boxes)

Fit by RANSAC, then refit on inliers via the covariance eigenproblem:

c = mean(𝒫_in)
Σ = cov(𝒫_in − c)
n = eigenvector of Σ with the smallest eigenvalue   (direction of least variance)

Only n is retained — it is scale-invariant, so a wrong global depth scale does not rotate it.

18.3 Resulting depth profile

D(v) ≈ h·f / (v − v_horizon)
Why tilt error is catastrophic in this mode. D diverges at the horizon, so a small error in n moves v_horizon and rescales the entire far field. A horizon placed above the frame compressed motorway traffic to 12–16 km/h.

19. Mode B — drone / nadir camera

Here n is known and f is unidentifiable.

n = [0, 0, −1]ᵀ        (exact — not estimated)
f := W_img             (arbitrary; see 19.2)

19.1 Collapse of the intersection

Since n·d = −1 identically:

t = −(h−z)/(−1) = h − z              ← independent of (u,v)

P = (h−z) · [ (u−cx)/f , (v−cy)/f , 1 ]ᵀ

The Z-component of every ground point is exactly h−z: depth is constant across the whole frame. Only the slant distance varies:

D(u,v) = (h−z) · √( ((u−cx)/f)² + ((v−cy)/f)² + 1 )

19.2 The degeneracy

x = (h−z)(u−cx)/f          y = (h−z)(v−cy)/f

h and f appear only as the ratio h/f. Therefore any (h, f) with the same ratio gives identical ground positions and identical speeds; fixing f arbitrarily and solving h from §20 recovers the correct ratio. Only D = ‖P‖ depends on the split, which a known altitude resolves:

f′ = f · H_true / h ,   h′ = H_true     ⟹    h′/f′ = h/f    (positions unchanged)
Formal reason the original constant worked overhead. A single METERS_PER_PIXEL = h/f is exactly the correct model for a nadir view — and only for a nadir view.

20. Scale inference — both modes

20.1 Measurement

For each tracked car, back-project the box's bottom corners onto the road plane using an assumed height h = 1, z = 0:

p_L = ground(x₁, y₂ | h=1),   p_R = ground(x₂, y₂ | h=1)
e   = p_R − p_L,              w₁ = ‖e‖

20.2 Admissibility gates

With Δ = c_t − c_(t−5) the ground displacement over 5 frames:

(i)   ‖Δ‖ ≥ 0.2 · w₁                        reject parked / crawling
(ii)  |Δ·e| / (‖Δ‖·w₁) < 0.5                box edge within 60° of ⊥ to travel

20.3 Solving for height

Ground distances scale linearly with h, and the box's lateral extremities lie on the body at height z_b, not on the road. Rays through them extended to z=0 spread by h/(h−z_b):

w₁ · h = W · h/(h − z_b)      ⟹      w₁ = W / (h − z_b)

                              ⟹      h = W / w₁ + z_b

20.4 Which statistic

For a car whose heading makes angle φ with the image horizontal, the axis-aligned box spans

extent(φ) = L·|cos φ| + W·|sin φ|   ≥   W ,    equality iff φ = 90°

The error is one-sided — contamination by vehicle length can only inflate w₁. The sample distribution has a hard floor at the truth with a tail above it, so:

h = W / percentile₂₅({w₁}) + z_b

21. Velocity estimation

Given the trajectory window {(tᵢ, xᵢ, yᵢ)}, i = 1…N, spanning T = t_N − t₁ ≈ 0.6 s:

S_tt = Σ (tᵢ − t̄)²
v_x  = Σ (tᵢ − t̄)(xᵢ − x̄) / S_tt              (likewise v_y)

speed   = 3.6 · √(v_x² + v_y²)                 [km/h]
heading = atan2(v_y, v_x)

Diagnostics carried alongside the estimate:

rᵢ = ‖ (xᵢ, yᵢ) − (v·tᵢ + b) ‖
r  = maxᵢ rᵢ                                    worst-case scatter [m]

se = 3.6 · √( se_x² + se_y² ),
     se_a = √( Σ r_aᵢ² / ((N−2) · S_tt) )       slope standard error [km/h]

22. Uncertainty model

Position noise. Detector jitter scales with box size, and box width scales as 1/D — the two effects cancel one power of D:

σ_px  ≈ κ · box_width = κ · W · f / D
σ_pos = D² · σ_px / (h·f) = κ · W · D / h        ← linear in D

Speed noise floor (no 1/√N term — jitter is correlated within a window):

σ_v = 3.6 · 2 σ_pos / T = 3.6 · 2 κ W D / (h T)   [km/h]

Combined uncertainty — the worse of measured scatter and geometric limit:

σ = max( se , σ_v )

23. Unreliability condition

A reading is reported UNRELIABLE if and only if:

    speed > 200                                 (A)  implausible
 ∨  r     > 1.5                                 (B)  track scatter / ID swap
 ∨  ( σ > 3.0  ∧  speed < 2·σ )                 (C)  below the noise floor

Otherwise:

speed < 3.0   →   "stopped"    (a valid measurement of zero)
else          →   "moving"     (report speed)
N < 4         →   "warmup"     (no claim yet)
(C) in words. It applies only when the noise floor exceeds the stopped threshold — i.e. when the measurement genuinely cannot separate stationary from moving slowly. Within that regime a reading must clear 2σ to be claimed. Failing (C) yields UNRELIABLE, never 0: collapsing those two states was the original bug.
