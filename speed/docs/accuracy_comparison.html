Drone vs Fixed Camera

Accuracy comparison of the two camera configurations in this system. All figures measured on the project's own footage and calibrations — none estimated.

1. Verdict first

The drone (overhead) configuration is measurably more accurate, and the reason is geometric rather than incidental: looking straight down, one pixel maps to the same ground distance everywhere in frame, and the camera sits ten times further from the road. Both effects work in its favour and neither depends on tuning.

The fixed camera's advantage is not accuracy — it is practicality: it can run unattended indefinitely, which a drone cannot.

3. Strengths and weaknesses

Drone / overhead

Advantages

  • Exact geometry. The ground normal is known, not estimated — the single largest error source in the fixed system does not exist here.
  • Uniform accuracy. A vehicle at the frame edge is measured as precisely as one at the centre (1.0× spread).
  • ~10× lower noise floor at equal target distance, because the camera is far from the road.
  • Effectively no occlusion — every vehicle is separately visible.
  • Wide, even coverage — 68 m across, versus a 12–26 m wedge.
  • No depth model. Faster calibration, ~7 GB less memory, one fewer thing to be wrong.
  • Benign failure mode — errors are uniform and easy to detect.

Disadvantages

  • Cannot run unattended. Battery limits sessions to tens of minutes — disqualifying for permanent enforcement.
  • Requires an aerial-trained detector. A COCO model detects essentially nothing from overhead (0/frame vs 23–28).
  • Hover drift must be corrected or parked cars acquire phantom speeds.
  • Small targets — vehicles occupy few pixels, so detection confidence must be lowered.
  • Assumes true nadir. Tilt is not measured, so an oblique clip in this mode yields confident nonsense (observed: 71.98 m, 90° tilt).
  • Regulatory limits on flight over traffic in many jurisdictions.

Fixed / oblique

Advantages

  • Runs indefinitely — the only configuration suitable for permanent, unattended enforcement.
  • No ego-motion. Rigid mounting removes an entire class of error.
  • Standard detectors work. Vehicles appear in the canonical aspect COCO models were trained on.
  • Large near-field targets — a close vehicle spans hundreds of pixels, giving excellent near-range precision.
  • Uses existing infrastructure — poles, overpasses, and installed CCTV.
  • Recognisable evidence frames — a side/front view is more identifiable than a roof.

Disadvantages

  • Accuracy varies 2.3× across the frame — the same vehicle measures differently depending on where it is.
  • Tilt must be estimated from a depth model, with no independent cross-check on its focal-length head.
  • Far-field errors amplify. Depth diverges at the horizon, so small tilt errors rescale distant readings badly.
  • Occlusion is routine — one truck can hide several vehicles.
  • 8% of readings withheld as below the noise floor, nearly all at range.
  • Needs Depth Pro — ~7 GB and ~13 s at calibration.
  • Lens distortion unmodelled, biasing measurements toward frame edges.

4. Why the drone is more accurate — the mechanism

Both effects follow from one formula. The speed noise floor in this system is

sigma_v  =  2 · JITTER_FRAC · W · d / (h · T) · 3.6      [km/h]

where d is target distance and h the camera height. Accuracy is governed by the ratio d/h, and the two configurations sit at opposite ends of it:

ConfigurationCamera height hTypical dd / hσv
Drone / overhead68.0 m70 m1.00.1 km/h
Fixed / oblique7.0 m40 m5.70.7 km/h

A drone at 68 m looking down has d/h ≈ 1; a 7 m pole looking along the road has d/h ≈ 6. The drone's altitude is its accuracy.

The uniformity point is the more important one. The fixed camera's 2.3× scale spread means a measurement's quality depends on where in the frame it happened — so a single accuracy figure for that camera is misleading, and per-reading uncertainty must be tracked. Overhead, one number describes the whole frame.

5. Honest limits of this comparison

  • Neither has been validated against a reference speed instrument. No radar or GPS ground truth was available. Accuracy here is inferred from geometric self-consistency (lane width, vanishing point, parked cars reading zero), which bounds error at roughly 5–10% but does not prove it.
  • Sample size is small — four scenes, three oblique and one nadir. Figures are indicative, not statistical.
  • The comparison is of these two configurations, not of drones versus fixed cameras in general. A 30 m gantry camera would sit much closer to the drone's numbers.
  • Four constants are empirically tuned (p25, JITTER_FRAC, SIGMA_TO_REPORT, z_b) on a handful of clips, and both configurations inherit any error in them.
  • The drone figures come from a hovering platform. A drifting or manually-flown drone would be materially worse.
